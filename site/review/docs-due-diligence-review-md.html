SYNTHETIC TRAINING CASE. HarborPay Demo, its service, documents, roles, facts and control states are fictional. This is an analyst-work-product example, not an audit, vendor attestation, legal opinion, or actual supplier decision. Assessment date: 16 November 2026. Prepared for a fictional payroll service owner; human approval is pending.

Decision in brief

Recommendation: hold production onboarding pending evidence and authorized review. The service is modelled as high criticality because it processes employee identity, payroll and bank-account data and supports payroll operations. That is a proposed lab tier, not a validated business classification. The present packet does not establish current, authentic assurance over the purchased payroll service, and the initial customer MFA fixture is disabled. No evidence here establishes a breach or a vendor control failure. Request a current in-scope independent report and verifiable certificate, or a security-reviewer-validated alternative evidence package proportionate to this service; an ISO certificate alone would not answer operational-control, contract, subprocessor or continuity questions. A conditional path may be considered only by an authorized business risk owner after contractual protections, minimum controls, compensating safeguards, dated actions and stop/escalation triggers are evaluated. Any exception requires that accountable human approval; it is not an automatic bypass.

Scope and method

The case concerns a payroll-processing SaaS handling employee identity, payroll and bank-account details. The review considers the actual service boundary, evidence currency/authenticity, shared responsibility, and treatment ownership. Framework references are question prompts mapped to selected NIST CSF 2.0 outcomes and ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A themes in case-study/control_catalog.json; they are not a full controls audit or compliance conclusion. Read the original synthetic exhibits in case-study/evidence-packet/.

Evidence interpretation

  • E01 — certificate claim: It states a validity period ending 31 October 2026, before this assessment, and lacks issuer, accreditation details, certificate number, authoritative verification, and underlying certificate. Its declared payroll wording is not authenticated. Record CERT-EXPIRY as stale/unverified assurance. Expiry does not prove a control failed, a breach happened, or the supplier is unsafe. Ask for current verifiable evidence and validate legal entity, exact scope/sites/exclusions, certification body, accreditation chain, validity/status and any suspension.
  • E02 v1 — Type II-style summary: Its stated 1 January–30 September 2026 period is historical. The only stated system is a customer support portal; payroll is explicitly excluded. Therefore it is out of scope for this review, irrespective of its recent-looking dates. The report itself, criteria, opinion, tests, exceptions and subservice/CUEC schedules are absent. “Not represented” means unknown, not no exceptions.
  • E02-v2 — simulated scope edit: The revised fixture lists payroll and removes the exclusion. It changes a declared field only. It cannot authenticate the report, prove payroll was in the original examined boundary, supply period testing, exceptions/opinion, or establish actual control operation. Keep ASSURANCE-SCOPE open for a real review even if a software fixture check changes state.
  • SOC 2 Type II considerations: A genuine Type II report describes a system and reports on controls over a period. Review the actual service-organization description, opinion, criteria, tests/results, exceptions and management responses. Establish whether named subservice organizations are included or carved out; for a carve-out, identify dependencies and review the service auditor reports and complementary subservice-organization controls as appropriate. Review customer CUECs as conditions for the service organization's controls to achieve their objectives. The exercise E03 MFA CUEC is separately assigned to the fictional customer; do not imply it appeared in a real report.
  • Bridge statement: The synthetic E02-B prompt describes management's hypothetical no-material-change claim from 1 October to 16 November. No signed letter or corroboration exists. Even if received, such a letter would not extend the assurance period, test controls in the gap, show absence of incidents, or replace the independent report. Verify authority, coverage, incidents, changes and downstream-provider changes; leave the gap explicit.
  • E03 — customer CUEC: The customer is responsible for MFA on privileged payroll-admin accounts; v1's mock state is disabled. This is a customer-side prerequisite, not a HarborPay finding. E03-v2 marks MFA enabled in a labelled mock only. A real check needs the full privileged identity population, enforcement settings, exceptions/break-glass treatment and dated evidence. Keep real-world implementation unverified.
  • E04 — access-policy fixture: It asserts policy presence, approval date (1 October) and payroll scope. It supplies no full controlled policy or samples. It cannot evidence that access reviews, least privilege, MFA or deprovisioning operated effectively.

Risk rationale and treatment register

Scenario: privileged credential compromise or malicious/erroneous change exposes payroll identities and bank details, or disrupts payroll processing. This is a plausible threat scenario based on data sensitivity and service dependency, not an observed threat or incident. Inherent likelihood: Medium (proposed qualitative rating) because a cloud payroll service has remote access and administrative dependencies, but the packet contains no threat intelligence, attack-surface evidence or control testing to calibrate probability. Inherent impact: High (proposed) due to sensitive financial/personnel data and payroll availability consequences; business impact and legal obligations require owner validation. These ratings are prioritization judgments, not calculated metrics.

Residual risk: unassessed. Current assurance, operational controls, actual customer configuration, contract terms, downstream parties and recovery evidence are insufficient to judge control effectiveness. Do not infer residual risk from the synthetic control flags or platform status.
